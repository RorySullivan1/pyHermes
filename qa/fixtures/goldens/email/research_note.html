Summary

Momentum has earned a premium in every market studied since the first evidence (Jegadeesh and Titman 1993), and it survives the four-factor model (Carhart 1997). Its cost is the momentum crash (Daniel and Moskowitz 2016).1
Key takeaways

The premium survives trading costs at moderate turnover, and the crash risk is what a position should be sized to.

Data and Method

Why skip a month

The latest month is skipped, because short-horizon returns reverse.

Stocks are ranked each month on their return over the prior twelve months, skipping the most recent (Jegadeesh and Titman 1993). The winner-minus-loser portfolio is set out in Exhibit 1, and the sample in Exhibit A.1.

Glossary

Momentum

The tendency of recent winners to keep outperforming recent losers.

Momentum crash

A sudden reversal in which past losers sharply outperform.

Turnover

The share of a portfolio traded each month to keep it on its signal.

Winner-minus-loser

A portfolio long the top decile of past returns and short the bottom decile.
