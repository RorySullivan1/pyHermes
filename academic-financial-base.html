Week in Review

Equity markets advanced for a third consecutive week as softer inflation data reinforced expectations of continued monetary easing. The S&P 500 gained 1.4%, led by cyclical sectors, while the Nasdaq posted a more modest 0.9% increase. Treasuries sold off modestly, with the 10-year yield rising 12 basis points to 4.28% amid heavier supply. Credit spreads tightened, with investment-grade spreads reaching their narrowest level since early 2022.

Key Themes

1.

Disinflation trend intact despite services stickiness

Core PCE printed at 2.6% year-over-year, in line with consensus. Goods deflation continues to offset persistent shelter inflation, supporting the Fed’s gradual approach to rate normalization.

2.

Breadth improving beneath the surface

The equal-weighted S&P 500 outperformed its cap-weighted counterpart by 40 basis points, the fourth consecutive week of broader participation. Small-cap indices also showed renewed relative strength.

3.

Credit spreads at cycle tights warrant vigilance

Investment-grade OAS compressed to 82 basis points, the tightest since early 2022. While fundamentals remain supportive, asymmetric downside risk argues for selective positioning within credit allocations.

Closing Remarks

The near-term path remains constructive for risk assets, supported by moderating inflation and resilient labor markets. We maintain a modest overweight to equities with a preference for quality and improving breadth, while staying neutral on duration given the repricing in rates.
